## Fairer Net Sales option

Use this for Section 3:

`"Net Sales" means the gross amounts invoiced by Company and actually collected from the applicable customer for sales covered by this Agreement, less only the following deductions to the extent actually incurred and specifically attributable to the applicable sale: (i) actual customer returns, refunds, and credits; (ii) customer-specific discounts or rebates actually granted and documented for that sale; (iii) sales, use, excise, and similar taxes separately stated on the applicable invoice and remitted to a governmental authority; and (iv) shipping or freight charges separately itemized to the customer on the applicable invoice. Net Sales shall not be reduced by internal allocations, overhead, marketing spend, slotting fees, commissions paid to others, bad-debt reserves, general write-offs, intercompany charges, or promotional allowances not specifically tied to the applicable customer sale. For clarity, because compensation is payable only on amounts actually collected, Company may not take any additional reserve or bad-debt deduction except for a specific refund, credit, or chargeback actually issued on the applicable sale.`

## Why this Net Sales clause is fair

- It still protects Company on real refunds, credits, taxes, and itemized freight.
- It blocks vague deductions that can quietly hollow out commission math.
- It avoids double-dipping on bad debt by tying compensation to actual collections and then forbidding extra reserve-style deductions on top.
- It keeps the accounting tied to the actual customer sale instead of general business expenses.
